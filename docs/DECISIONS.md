# Design decisions

A running record of why things are the way they are: what was chosen, why, and what the alternative was. Newest at the bottom. "Owner" says who made the call: **Kevin** decided it, or **Claude** proposed it and it stands until challenged.

| # | Date | Decision | Why | Alternative considered | Owner |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 13 Oct 2026 | Cover EU, Spanish and UK data-protection and AI law, in English and Spanish, starting with Tier 1 documents | A corpus large enough to make retrieval choices matter; the EU/UK differences and changing law make it a harder problem than a single-law chatbot | A single-law assistant (the common case) | Kevin |
| 2 | 13 Oct 2026 | Code under Apache-2.0; the texts keep their own licences (see `SOURCES.md`) | Patent grant, common in AI projects; keeps code and data licences clearly apart | MIT | Kevin |
| 3 | 13 Oct 2026 | Chat model: Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite, on a billing-enabled project for any public demo | Google's terms require paid services for apps available in the EEA, Switzerland and the UK; the model is cheap (about $0.0015 a question, estimated) | Open-weight models through a free host | Kevin |
| 4 | 13 Oct 2026 | Deploy on Azure Container Apps with the image in GitHub Container Registry | Same free monthly grant as Cloud Run; Azure Container Registry has no free tier; adds a second cloud | Cloud Run (already known) | Kevin |
| 5 | 13 Oct 2026 | An answer-model comparison is part of the plan (roadmap item 4.6) | Measure whether the default model is good enough instead of assuming | None: Flash-Lite only | Kevin |
| 6 | 14 Oct 2026 | `src/` layout, one package (`law_assistant`), one test file per module, Python 3.12 in a `.venv` | Tests import the installed package, so they cannot pass by accident from the wrong folder; 3.12 is widely supported by AI libraries | Flat layout; Python 3.14 (newest, less library support) | Claude |
| 7 | 14 Oct 2026 | Test first for deterministic code; ruff for lint and format; CI runs lint, tests and a secret scan | Fetchers, parsers and chunkers have exact right answers, so tests can be written before the code | Tests after the code | Claude |
| 8 | 14 Oct 2026 | The HTTP client is the standard library behind a small `Client` interface; time and HTTP are passed in | No runtime dependency yet; tests use a fake client and a fake clock, so they never touch the network or sleep | `requests` or `httpx` | Claude |
| 9 | 14 Oct 2026 | Fetched documents go to `data/raw/` (git-ignored) with a `.meta.json` record: URL, retrieval time, hash, size, licence, credit, User-Agent | The sources have different licences and some must not be redistributed; the record is the "retrieval date" that `SOURCES.md` promises | Committing the raw files | Claude |
| 10 | 14 Oct 2026 | A document is fetched once and never again unless forced; writes are atomic (temporary file, then rename) | Fewer requests to public services; a failed or interrupted download can never leave a half-written file that looks valid | Always re-fetch | Claude |
| 11 | 14 Oct 2026 | Politeness rules in code: identifying User-Agent (a missing contact is an error), robots.txt honoured, at least 5 seconds between requests or the site's crawl delay if slower, http(s) URLs only | legislation.gov.uk requires an identifying agent and sets a 5-second crawl delay; the rules are also enforced by tests | Hand-written discipline | Claude |
| 12 | 14 Oct 2026 | robots.txt: a 4xx answer means no restrictions, any other failure stops the fetch | That is what RFC 9309 specifies; failing closed on server errors avoids ignoring rules we could not read | Treat every failure as "allowed" | Claude |
| 13 | 14 Oct 2026 | A small hand-written `.env` reader instead of a library | Four lines of behaviour, tested; avoids a dependency | `python-dotenv` | Claude |
| 14 | 14 Oct 2026 | EU acts are fetched from the Cellar API as XHTML, by CELEX number, with the language chosen by header | EUR-Lex's web pages answer scripts with a bot challenge, which we do not bypass; XHTML keeps stable ids for articles and recitals that the parser can use | Formex XML or PDF (harder to parse); scraping the website | Claude |
| 15 | 14 Oct 2026 | Keep both the Official Journal text and the consolidated text of each EU act | Recitals exist only in the Official Journal text; the consolidated text shows the law as currently amended and gives the as-of versions (AI Act: original 2024, consolidated 27 July 2026) | Consolidated only (loses the recitals) | Claude |
| 16 | 14 Oct 2026 | Every parser outputs one common record, `Provision` (id, instrument, jurisdiction, language, version, unit, kind, path, text, heading, chapter, order), saved as JSON Lines in `data/parsed/` | Chunking, retrieval and citations then work on one shape whatever the source markup; the path gives exact citations such as Article 6(1)(f) | One format per source | Claude |
| 17 | 14 Oct 2026 | The EU parser reads the XHTML with the standard-library XML parser and keeps structure from the markup's own ids (`art_6`, `rct_47`, `anx_III`) | The files are well-formed XML and the ids are stable; no HTML-parsing dependency needed | BeautifulSoup or lxml | Claude |
| 18 | 14 Oct 2026 | A parse is trusted only after structural checks: unique ids, no empty leaves, numbering without gaps, known totals (GDPR 99 articles and 173 recitals, AI Act 113 and 180 and 13 annexes, Omnibus 4 and 47), clean text, identical English and Spanish skeletons, and coverage of the source text | A parser fails quietly; these checks found six real markup differences before any text was used (see `docs/PARSING.md`) | Manual spot-checks only | Claude |
| 19 | 14 Oct 2026 | Tests on the real downloaded files are skipped when the files are absent (CI); small fixtures taken from the real markup carry the logic tests | CI never needs the raw data, which is git-ignored, and the real-file tests still run on any machine that has fetched it | Committing the raw files; mocking everything | Claude |
| 20 | 14 Oct 2026 | The Digital Omnibus (an amending act) is parsed with the same code: quoted blocks are read as text of their amendment point, data tables are kept row by row, and language-specific labels such as "14a" and "14 bis" are not compared between languages | The Omnibus matters for its dates and changes, not for a perfect article skeleton; nothing in it is lost (coverage 100.0%) | A separate parser for amending acts | Claude |
| 21 | 14 Oct 2026 | The line-length lint rule is relaxed for tests only | Long expected strings in tests are clearer unwrapped | Wrapping them | Claude |
| 22 | 14 Oct 2026 | The consolidated EU texts get their own parser, sharing only the id builder and the checks with the Official Journal parser | The two markups share nothing but the article ids (different class names, two-column grids instead of tables, amendment marks inside the text) | One parser with two modes | Claude |
| 23 | 14 Oct 2026 | Both versions of each EU text are kept side by side, labelled in the `version` field (`oj`, `consolidated-20160504`, `consolidated-20260727`), not merged | Recitals exist only in the Official Journal text; the consolidated text is the law as amended; the as-of logic needs both | Keep only the consolidated text | Claude |
| 24 | 14 Oct 2026 | A consolidated text is cross-checked against the Official Journal text id by id, ignoring spacing: every difference must be an amendment or a corrigendum. For the AI Act, every changed article must be one the Omnibus mentions | An independent check that the consolidated parse is right: the GDPR matches exactly apart from the 2018 corrigendum (16 wording changes in English, 38 in Spanish) | Spot-checking | Claude |
| 25 | 14 Oct 2026 | Language-dependent labels for inserted provisions (English 14a, Spanish "14 bis") are stored as written and normalised only when comparing the two languages | The stored ids stay faithful to each language's official numbering; the comparison still works | Normalising in the stored ids | Claude |
| 26 | 15 Oct 2026 | The UK parser takes each provision's place from the XML's own citation ids (`article-6-1-a`, `section-6A-2`, `schedule-1-paragraph-3-2-a`) | The ids are the official citations, so no numbering logic of ours can disagree with them | Counting and labelling levels ourselves | Claude |
| 27 | 15 Oct 2026 | Repealed and not-yet-in-force provisions are left out of the current text and counted; the only article or section numbers allowed to be missing are exactly those | The assistant must not quote law that is not in force; the check proves nothing else was dropped (UK GDPR Articles 60 to 76, 92 and 93; ten sections of the new Act not yet in force) | Including them with a flag | Claude |
| 28 | 15 Oct 2026 | `in_force_from` is the date from which the current version of the element, or of the text around it, applies (`RestrictStartDate`), not the date its own wording changed | It is what the source states; it agrees with the government's commencement table (Article 17(1)(g) 31 March 2026, section 103 19 June 2026); the later as-of logic can refine it | Leaving it empty | Claude |
| 29 | 15 Oct 2026 | The Provision model gains the units `section` and `schedule`, for the UK's sections and schedules | UK citations are "section 6" and "Schedule 1 paragraph 3", not articles and annexes; the UK GDPR keeps `article` and `annex` as in its text | Mapping sections to articles | Claude |
