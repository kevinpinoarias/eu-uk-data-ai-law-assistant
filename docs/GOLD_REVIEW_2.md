# The second independent review of the gold set (15 October 2026)

Decision 67 promised a second round of independent review near the end, before the final write-up and before the 40 test questions are read. This is that round, what it found, what was checked, and what changed. The label on the set is unchanged: **drafted by an AI assistant, checked by scripts, independently reviewed by AI passes (now ten in two rounds), and not reviewed by a qualified lawyer.**

## How it was done

Five separate AI reviewers, each given a different 16 of the 80 questions (questions dealt out in id order so every reviewer saw every type and language). Each saw only: the question, the expected answer, the gold labels (which provisions a search should find, primary or acceptable, pinpoint ids, version) and the text of those provisions as held in our corpus. Not the drafting notes, not the chunks' date metadata, not any experiment result. Each was told to be skeptical, to judge only from the text, to mark anything that rests on outside knowledge as such, and could edit nothing. Packets were generated by script from the frozen set and the parsed corpus.

## What was verified before anything was adopted

Every finding that could change the set was checked against the corpus. Three groups did not hold, or did not matter:

- **The three "major" findings (q049, q050, q051) are not errors in the gold.** Each is a "since when" question whose date (5 February 2026, 10 May 2023, 31 March 2026) the reviewers could not find in the cited text. The date is real: it is the chunk's `in_force_from` metadata, taken from the sources' own dates, and the questions' notes say so; the reviewers' packets left that metadata out. **They did expose a real gap in the answer pipeline**: it never showed the writer that date, which is why q049 was declined and q051 was judged "partly" in the first development run. See "What it changed in the system" below.
- **A suspected corpus bug was not one.** Reviewers noticed that the Spanish GDPR as first published lacks "sin dilación indebida" in Article 12(3) while the consolidated text has it, and thought the parser had dropped it. It is the 2018 corrigendum: the project's parsing notes already record 38 Spanish wording changes of that kind, and the English text has the phrase in both versions. The questions that rest on that phrase (q023, q035, q055) carry the consolidated wording and a gold version of "any". Likewise Article 6 is complete in every version and language in the parsed corpus (one reviewer's reading of it was cut short).
- **Most label comments do not affect any score.** A gold provision counts as one target and is found if a chunk contains any of its pinpoint ids, so adding or removing a pinpoint inside a provision does not change how many provisions a question needs, and the metrics never look at the quotations (they are checked by script against the cited text in any of its languages). So the many comments of the kind "this pinpoint is not needed", "this sub-paragraph is not listed", or "this quotation is in English but the text shown is Spanish" were noted and left alone: changing them after the development results were known could only look like tuning the labels.

## What was changed (gold version 2)

Ten questions and one table caveat. Each change is recorded in that question's `notes` field, the manifest records version 2 with version 1's fingerprint, and CI checks the new fingerprint.

|---|---|---|---|
| q044 | dev | removed the acceptable consolidated Article 113(c)(i) | its dates (2 December 2027) are wrong for a question dated 1 January 2026, so retrieving it should not count as relevant |
| q046 | test | removed the acceptable consolidated Article 4a | its text post-dates the question's date and the question asks about the original wording |
| q076 | test | Article 101 promoted to primary; Article 113 lead-in added as a pinpoint | the second half of the question depends on Article 101 and on the general date in the lead-in |
| q045 | test | fixed "educación y formación el contexto" to "..., así como el contexto" | a missing connective made the Spanish ungrammatical |

| q080 | dev | removed "a breach detected yesterday may already be near the deadline" | a statement about the user's own facts, which the answer is told not to make |
| q009 | dev | "other forms of scoring are not prohibited by Article 5" narrowed to "by this case of Article 5" | Article 5 has other prohibitions |
| q019 | dev | removed a sentence on what happens to a model without general-purpose generality | the cited provisions do not state it |

**Effect on results.** On the development half, BGE-M3's recall@5 is 0.532 and MRR 0.462 under both versions; nDCG@10 moves from 0.478 to 0.476. The test half has never been scored for retrieval. Results already logged were obtained under version 1; the answer-layer results will be re-run under version 2 (they need the model's daily allowance anyway), and the correctness figures of version 1 should not be compared with those.

## Left unchanged, and why

- **Quotations in English against Spanish text** (about 18 questions): no metric reads them; a future clean-up could translate them.
- **Missing pinpoints and role disagreements inside a provision** (about 25 questions): no scoring effect, see above.
- **Expected answers that omit a condition or an exception the cited text has** (about 15 questions, for example the 49(1) conditions in q022 or the research safeguards in q032): the expected answers are labelled reference statements of the key points, not exhaustive treatments, and the judge compares key content.
- **Statements resting on outside knowledge**: not adoptable; several are probably correct but the corpus cannot show them.
- **Refusal questions whose expected answer also explains a rule** (q064, q065, q080): the evaluation only checks that the system refuses and gives the right reason, as the set's design says. A reviewer is right that a reader could mistake the expected answer for a requirement to answer; what is scored is stated here and in `GOLD_SET.md`.
- **q077** (a terse question where the right answer gives the EU and the UK figure): the table has a row for each, but the question can name only one key. Left as a known limit.
- **q039** ("En principio no" for a worker's duty to answer after hours): a reviewer thinks the text does not say the employer cannot demand replies. This is a judgement about strength of wording that I did not verify further and did not change.

## What it changed in the system

The reviewers' three "major" findings were all dates that live in metadata. The answer layer now has an option (off by default, so the measured configuration is reproducible) that adds "in force from <date>" to each passage's header and a rule for using it (decision 98). It is built and tested, measured together with the version-aware search of decision 90 as variant D, and waiting for the model's daily allowance.

## Limits of this review

Ten AI passes are not a lawyer. The reviewers share a family with the writer of the answers, they could only check against the text they were shown, and none of them knew what a retrieval system would do with the labels. The set is therefore better, not certified.
